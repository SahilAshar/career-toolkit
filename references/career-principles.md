# Career Principles Reference

Universal, research-validated principles for resume writing and professional positioning. These apply across fields — not just tech. Skills reference this file for grounding feedback in evidence rather than vibes.

## The XYZ Formula

**"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"** — Google SVP Laszlo Bock.

Universal consensus across every major career resource. Every bullet should follow this structure: **Action verb + what was built/delivered/achieved + quantified impact.**

Field examples:
- **Engineering:** "Designed a real-time data pipeline processing 2B events/day, reducing alert latency from 5 minutes to 30 seconds"
- **Marketing:** "Launched a product rebrand across 12 markets, increasing brand recall by 34% in 6 months"
- **Nursing:** "Implemented a patient handoff protocol across 4 units, reducing medication errors by 22%"
- **Finance:** "Built a forecasting model for $400M revenue portfolio, improving accuracy from 82% to 94%"
- **Education:** "Redesigned AP Biology curriculum for 300+ students, raising exam pass rate from 61% to 78%"

## Verb Tiers

Ranked by signal strength. Higher tiers signal ownership and strategic thinking; lower tiers signal execution and collaboration. The right mix depends on target level.

### Tier 1 — Ownership & Strategy
Led, Designed, Built, Owned, Drove, Architected, Founded, Launched, Established, Pioneered

### Tier 2 — Measurable Impact
Reduced, Increased, Optimized, Accelerated, Scaled, Migrated, Eliminated, Grew, Improved, Doubled

### Tier 3 — Influence & Collaboration
Coordinated, Partnered, Aligned, Standardized, Mentored, Trained, Advised, Facilitated

**Guidance:** Senior/lead roles should lean Tier 1-2. Early/mid-career roles naturally have more Tier 2-3. Avoid starting every bullet with the same verb.

## Level Signals

How to differentiate career levels through language. These patterns are universal across fields — only the nouns change.

| Signal | Early/Mid Career | Senior/Lead | Director/Executive |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Problem scope** | Team-level tasks | Cross-team, department-wide | Org-wide, company-wide |
| **Direction** | Receives problems, delivers solutions | Identifies problems worth solving | Sets strategy, defines what problems exist |
| **Ownership** | "Contributed to," "Supported" | "Owned," "Led," "Drove" | "Defined," "Established," "Transformed" |
| **Impact framing** | "Improved X by Y%" | "Designed X that enabled Y across Z teams" | "Set the strategy that drove $Xm in outcome" |
| **People signals** | N/A | "Mentored 3 engineers" | "Built and led a team of 15" |

## Resume vs. Interview Boundary

### On the resume
Scope, ownership, results, scale anchors, quantified outcomes. Technology/tool names serve as keywords and pattern-matching signals. Keep it factual and scannable.

### In interviews (NOT on the resume)
Reasoning, tradeoff analysis ("chose X over Y because Z"), failure stories, conflict resolution narratives. These belong in behavioral answers and case studies, not on paper.

**Why this matters:** Putting reasoning on the resume wastes space and robs the opportunity to demonstrate live thinking. Naming a specific approach already signals to a senior interviewer that alternatives were considered — they'll ask why, and that's where differentiation happens.

Sources: interviewing.io (scan time data), Levels.fyi STAR method guide, formation.dev, Tech Interview Handbook.

## Bullet Structure

- **1 line target, 2 lines absolute max** per bullet
- **Start every bullet with an action verb** (present tense for current role, past tense for previous)
- **No pronouns** ("I," "we," "my")
- **60-70% of bullets should have hard metrics.** The rest can use scope anchors: "across 4 departments," "serving 200+ clients," "12-person team"
- **Don't force metrics.** A scope anchor is better than a made-up number. Credibility matters more than completeness.
- **Bullet count by recency:**
  - Current role: 4-5 bullets
  - Previous role: 3-4 bullets
  - Older roles: 1-2 bullets
- **One page, always** (unless 15+ years experience and applying for executive roles)

## Anti-Patterns

Language that weakens a resume regardless of field:

| Anti-Pattern | Why It's Bad | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| "Responsible for..." | Passive, describes a job description not an achievement | Start with an action verb + result |
| "Assisted with..." / "Helped with..." | Signals junior/support role | Own the contribution: what did YOU do? |
| "Various" / "multiple" | Vague — how many? | Use a number or name them |
| "Successfully completed..." | Redundant — of course it was successful if it's on your resume | Cut "successfully," lead with the verb |
| Listing tools without context | "Used Salesforce" vs. "Built a Salesforce pipeline that..." | Frame tools as HOW, not WHAT |
| Delivery speed as primary signal | "Delivered in 6 weeks" reads as executor, not leader | Reframe as impact: what did the speed enable? |
| Forced metrics | "Improved team morale by 15%" — how? | Use scope anchors when metrics aren't credible |
| Job-description bullets | "Managed a team of 5 analysts" | What did the team ACHIEVE under your leadership? |

## LinkedIn vs. Resume

LinkedIn and resumes serve different purposes:

| Dimension | Resume | LinkedIn |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Tone** | Formal, scannable, ATS-optimized | Conversational, confident, human |
| **Length** | 1 page (strict) | No limit, but brevity = confidence |
| **Structure** | Bullet points, strict formatting | Short paragraphs, narrative flow |
| **Goal** | Pass the 6-second recruiter scan + ATS | Build a professional brand, attract inbound |
| **Metrics** | Essential (60-70% of bullets) | Nice to have, not required for every role |
| **Voice** | Third-person implied (no pronouns) | First-person is fine ("I lead...", "I build...") |

### LinkedIn Style Principles (Universal)
- Describe what you own, not what you did — scope is self-evident
- Let numbers and context carry weight — don't explain things the reader already understands
- Brevity = confidence — if it can be one sentence, make it one sentence
- Two sentences per role is ideal: (1) what you own + scale, (2) the hardest/most interesting thing you did
- Partner/client/product names build credibility — drop them naturally

## Keyword Strategy

### For ATS (resumes)
- Mirror language from target job postings
- Include both acronyms and spelled-out forms ("SEO / Search Engine Optimization")
- Place keywords in context (bullets), not just a skills list
- Skills sections should list tools/technologies/certifications the candidate can defend in an interview

### For LinkedIn
- Headline is the most important keyword real estate
- "Open to work" preferences use keywords for matching
- Endorsements and skills section affect search ranking
- Write for humans first, algorithms second
